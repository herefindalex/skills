# From Company Understanding to Role Hypotheses

Connect what the evidence supports, why it affects this role, which tradeoffs the person may face, and how to test the interpretation. [T01]

## Five dimensions

| Dimension | What to examine | Distinction to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome responsibility | Observable outcomes such as delivery, reliability, business capability, and staff development | Responsibility does not establish decision authority. |
| Decision authority | Architecture, priorities, hiring, releases, budget, and processes | Title or seniority does not establish authority. |
| Resources | Existing team, additional hiring, budget, and tools | Funding does not establish allocated resources. |
| Collaboration dependencies | Explicit relationships with product, sales, platform, finance, founders, and others | Collaboration does not establish reporting lines. |
| Success conditions | Changes the role is expected to produce | Do not invent 90-day commitments or evaluation systems. |

A JD describes expectations during recruitment, not a guarantee of actual conditions after joining. Unknown authority may be the most valuable interview question.

## Hypothesis card: six fields

Use this structure for each important hypothesis. Usually select two to four; use fewer when evidence is thin.

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis | A role interpretation that can be overturned or revised; explicitly label inference. |
| Evidence | Claims supported by sources actually read, with material locations; a short shareable evidence summary is enough. |
| Work implications | Specific responsibilities, collaboration, resources, or tradeoffs rather than generic workplace pressure. |
| Alternative explanation or counterevidence | Another situation that would change what the role means. |
| Support level | Follow [Evidence rules](evidence-rules.md), with a brief rationale rather than probability. |
| Validation question | A neutral question that elicits a concrete example; the answer must be able to change the interpretation. |

A hypothesis explains the role rather than privately diagnosing a manager. Generic phrases such as improving processes, working with founders, or end-to-end ownership directly support only the stated responsibilities. They do not establish uncontrolled delivery, unwillingness to delegate, or an incapable team. [T10]

## Derive challenges from product constraints

Use company-specific product promises to identify engineering or collaboration tradeoffs. A shared product serving multiple customers plus enterprise onboarding may require boundaries between customization and shared capabilities. Offline synchronization plus traceable changes may require handling retransmission and responsibility tracking. These are constraint-based inferences, not evidence of existing failures.

Each challenge must explain why it applies to this company and role. If removing the company name makes the text fit any vacancy, return to the sources. Without additional clues, reduce certainty around generic wording rather than inventing a story. [T02/T10]

Look for positive evidence and counterevidence without manufacturing risks for apparent balance. Missing organizational details in public material mean unconfirmed, not nonexistent. [T11]

## Multiple roles and analogies

Roles at one company may share business context, but their hypotheses and responsibility tables remain separate. A Staff Engineer's architecture responsibility cannot be transferred into an Engineering Manager's actual authority. [R03]

For peer comparisons, state which dimensions match and which do not. Comparisons identify common constraints; they do not replace evidence about the target. [T16]

## Do not tailor company needs to the candidate

Understand the company before reasoning backward from resume strengths about its needs. A supplied resume does not automatically call for a match percentage or a customized resume. Candidate fit requires a separate explicit request and is not this skill's main output. [T15]

## Companies with little information

Keep directly known product direction and JD expectations. Form at most one or two useful hypotheses, and leave revenue, team, authority, and other unsupported facts unknown. Caution should not reduce the whole report to unconfirmed statements. [T02]
